7. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND FOR THE γ SCORE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

[Jensen's inequality — E[γ] ≤ √E[γ²] since √· is concave]
Jensen, J.L.W.V.
"Sur les fonctions convexes et les inégalités entre les valeurs moyennes."
Acta Mathematica 30, 175–193 (1906).
DOI: 10.1007/BF02418571
Note: use this to justify reporting and optimising γ² (whose null hypothesis
E[γ²]=1 is exact) rather than γ (whose null E[γ]≈0.8 follows from Jensen).

[Error propagation — classical framework your score extends]
Taylor, J.R.
"An Introduction to Error Analysis: The Study of Uncertainties in Physical
Measurements." 2nd ed.
University Science Books, Sausalito, CA (1997).
ISBN: 978-0-935702-75-0

[Size-extensivity — the property your γ score is designed to satisfy]
Bartlett, R.J.
"Many-body perturbation theory and coupled cluster theory for electron
correlation in molecules."
Annual Review of Physical Chemistry 32, 359–401 (1981).
DOI: 10.1146/annurev.pc.32.100181.002043
Note: size-extensivity originates in quantum chemistry as the requirement that
the energy of N non-interacting systems scales linearly with N. Your γ satisfies
the analogous property: for statistically independent errors, E[γ²]=1 regardless
of reaction size N.

[Cosine similarity / vector geometry — the θ interpretation of γ]
γ = √N·|cos θ| is the projection of the normalised error vector onto the
all-ones direction. No single citation needed; standard linear algebra.
If you want a reference: Strang, G. "Linear Algebra and Its Applications."
4th ed. Thomson Brooks/Cole (2006).

[Law of total variance / variance of a sum — statistical basis of γ²]
Var(Σᵢ cᵢ δᵢ) vs Σᵢ cᵢ² Var(δᵢ): γ² = 1 when errors are uncorrelated,
γ² > 1 when correlated. Standard probability theory.
Casella, G. & Berger, R.L. "Statistical Inference." 2nd ed.
Duxbury Press (2002). ISBN: 978-0-534-24312-8

THEORETICAL CHAIN FOR THE PAPER
  γ² null hypothesis (E[γ²]=1 for uncorrelated errors, §7 Law of total variance)
  → Jensen inequality explains E[γ] ≈ 0.8 ≠ 1.0 (§7 Jensen)
  → size-extensivity of γ (§7 Bartlett, by analogy)
  → normalised two-task loss with fixed λ (§6 Kendall / GradNorm for context)

WHAT TO CITE FOR THE LOSS NORMALISATION
  The Kendall and GradNorm references (§6) let you acknowledge that more
  sophisticated balancing methods exist while justifying your simpler approach.
  The key argument: normalising by initial loss makes λ a genuine fractional
  weight without needing learned parameters or gradient statistics.
